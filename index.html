Introduction

The Start

  • You are going back to 600 CE. Known as the 'colonial era', 'late classical' and 'early medival'
  • We will go on this adventure through the colonial era and finally stop at free India
  • We first start in north India. The mighty Gupta empire has fallen. You are a young traveller setting off on an adventure across the sub-continent.
  • Our first stop is in Kannauj, a city by River Ganga where a powerfull king hosts poets and scholars.
  • A weeks later you reach the chalukya kingdom in Deccan where you see magnicifent temples and lively cities.
  • In the far south the Pallavas are carving entire temples out of rock a Mamallapuram(also known as Mahabalipuram), While in bengal the Palas are supporting great universities like Vikramshila
  • At the time you wont see any large empires but you see strong kingdoms
  • As you travel you see something different - a land buzzing with new ideas, art and culture, different in each region yet still all part of one India

The Great King of Kings

  • As we look back at northern India at this time we find remarkable ruler, Harshavardhana, ascending the throne in 606 CE
  • He belonged to the pushyabhuti(or Vardhana) dyanasty
  • The dyanasties earlier capital was sthaneshwar
  • Ruling from Kannauj(Anciently known as Kanyakubja, In present day Uttarpradesh) Harshavardhana expanded his empire over large parts of India. All though its precise extent is not known with certainity
  • Harsha was also a fine poet and Dramatist. He is assumed to be the author of 3 plays composed in classic sanskrit
  • They weave love stories with country-life, or themes of sacrifice and other high ethical values
  • While these are 'stories' historians use this to extract intresting details about the culture and society of those timesd - from systems of governance to social diversity, technolgies, foods and clothing
  • Harsha also partitionised scholars like Banabhutti(also known as Bana) who wrote Kadambari a beautiful literary work and one of the world's first novel. Bana also composed a biography of Harsha titled 'The Harshacharita'
  • Inscriptions depict Harsha as a devotee of shiva according to other sources he was also deeply attached to buddhism, while also respecting all schools of thought and belife
